# Evidence standard

## Strength of claim
- **Confirmed observation:** visible in an original supplied item (e.g., message asks for a training fee).
- **Primary-source corroborated:** employer/staffing agency official page or other authoritative statement confirms/refutes a particular practice.
- **Independent corroboration:** distinct reliable sources document same indicator or pattern.
- **Unverified allegation:** forum post, social-media complaint, screenshot repost or unattributed claim.
- **Inference:** analytical conclusion based on above; label as such.
- **Unknown:** no reliable supporting material.

## Risk classification (qualitative)
- **Critical:** direct financial/credential/device compromise demand or multiple well-supported signs indicating imminent harm.
- **High:** several mutually reinforcing deceptive indicators, or a substantial contradiction involving claimed identity and recruitment conditions.
- **Moderate:** meaningful concerns but incomplete evidence or plausible benign explanations.
- **Low:** checked indicators are broadly consistent with independent official sources; *not* a guarantee.
- **Inconclusive:** evidence is inadequate to classify.

## Confidence (independent from risk)
- **High:** source artifacts plus independently verifiable primary-source / strong corroboration.
- **Medium:** good original artifacts, partial independent corroboration.
- **Low:** incomplete or unverified evidence, no access to verification.

Never equate “high risk” with proven criminal wrongdoing. Record investigation date, which checks were performed, what wasn't checked, and the distinction between an official domain and an impersonator-controlled account.
